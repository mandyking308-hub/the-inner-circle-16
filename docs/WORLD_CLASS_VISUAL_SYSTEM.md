# Project Table — World-Class Visual System

## The design decision

Project Table should not look like a generic SaaS dashboard, a bank, or a fake-luxury black-and-gold members club.

The visual idea is:

> **A private journal, a Mayfair house and a family-office command centre in one system.**

The public experience should create desire, trust and curiosity. The private experience should become quieter, denser and more operational — a place a member can genuinely work.

## Competitive lessons

### TIGER 21 / FOX / Campden
Borrow:
- authority
- clear ideas
- strong peer proposition
- research / intelligence
- serious language around family, stewardship and legacy

Avoid:
- corporate conference-site feeling
- over-reliance on stock meeting photography
- dense institutional navigation

### Quintessentially
Borrow:
- service made tangible
- time-saving as a core benefit
- one relationship point
- global usefulness
- aspirational imagery

Avoid:
- lifestyle-for-lifestyle's-sake
- luxury-brand cliché
- a member experience dominated by consumption

### Soho House / The Arts Club
Borrow:
- warmth
- strong sense of place
- visual confidence
- editorial storytelling
- asymmetry and image-led composition
- culture rather than corporate polish

Avoid:
- making physical premises the whole value proposition
- decorative excess that hides the product

### The Conduit
Borrow:
- a clear point of view
- programming with purpose
- community identity
- members participating rather than consuming

Avoid:
- broad community language without enough individual operating value

## Brand character

Project Table should feel:
- intelligent, not academic
- wealthy, not showy
- warm, not beige
- private, not secretive
- international, not placeless
- editorial, not fashionable
- operational, not software-y
- multigenerational, not old-fashioned

## Colour system

Core:
- Parchment — primary warm ground
- Ink — principal text / private-workspace contrast
- Oxblood — signature brand colour; conviction, London, leather-bound journals
- Bronze — functional accent / metadata / progress
- Forest — secondary deep colour for family, stewardship and place
- Linen — cards and quiet surfaces
- Walnut — occasional warm dark neutral

Rules:
- No gold gradients.
- No glossy black luxury styling.
- Oxblood appears in small deliberate doses on the public site and as a functional highlight privately.
- Ink sections create drama; parchment sections create calm.
- Bronze is a detail colour, not decoration everywhere.

## Typography

Display: **Newsreader**
- editorial
- serious
- contemporary rather than bridal / traditional luxury

UI / body: **Manrope**
- highly legible
- clean enough for dense private tools
- does not compete with the editorial display face

Large editorial headlines should use tight line-height and generous negative space.

## Photography / imagery decision

Do not steal or imitate competitor photography.

For MVP:
- original commissioned-style SVG editorial illustrations in repository
- full-bleed crops, overlapping frames and image captions
- diagrams that feel like pages from a beautiful private journal
- visual motifs: table, doorway, passport, ledger, map, family tree, compass, decision board

Later:
- commission a real documentary photographer for London rooms, people at work, hands, objects and city detail
- avoid obvious handshakes, champagne, supercars, jets and generic skyscraper wealth imagery

## Public layout grammar

Use:
- asymmetric 5/7 and 7/5 grids
- large full-width editorial statements
- image + caption compositions
- horizontal rules
- numbered systems
- small upper-case metadata
- pull quotes / manifesto lines
- fewer boxes
- fewer six-card grids
- deliberate colour-section transitions

The homepage should feel like a magazine cover opening into a serious institution.

## Private product grammar

The private product should feel like the inside of a family-office notebook:
- dark ink navigation rail
- parchment workspace
- restrained oxblood active states
- task / decision / owner language
- less marketing copy
- clear hierarchy
- dense enough to work in, calm enough to think in

The flagship Life Decision Room must look materially more operational than the public marketing pages.

## Signature moments

1. **Hero collage** — the private room + Decision Room overlaid like two pages on a desk.
2. **The line** — “Success creates complexity. Nobody hands you the map.”
3. **Decision Room preview** — four lanes visible on the homepage.
4. **Family continuum** — learning → work → leadership → stewardship.
5. **Member service stack** — peers, specialists, concierge and evidence shown as one connected system.
6. **Oxblood action** — all primary public actions use one distinctive brand signal.

## Navigation decision

Do not expose every feature in the main header.

Primary public navigation:
- The Table
- Decision Room
- Families
- Trusted Partners
- Concierge
- Membership

Secondary routes live in the footer / ecosystem.

## Tone

Short, assured, concrete.

Prefer:
- “Stop collecting advice. Start closing decisions.”
- “Advice is useful. Finished is better.”
- “Skill is not execution.”
- “A move is never just a visa.”

Avoid:
- “exclusive luxury lifestyle”
- “unparalleled world-class ecosystem”
- “elite network”
- empty superlatives

The product should demonstrate the claim instead.
